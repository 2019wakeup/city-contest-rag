## A B S T R A C T

This study aims to investigate resilience differences of asphalt pavements across China’s climate zones in response to climate change. The impacts of climate change on rutting depth (RD), influence degree on rutting resilience, and Greenhouse Gas emissions in various climate zones were evaluated, and adaptation measures were proposed. The findings indicate that the warmer climate zones will be significantly affected. Freeways in these zones, serving for 10 years under projected climate conditions 40 years ahead, are expected to have RD increments exceeding 2 mm. Carbon dioxide equivalent emissions could increase by 23.1 million tons (a 39.4% increase) compared to current conditions. The degree of influence of climate change on rutting resilience will significantly differentiate among the climate zones. Four potential adaptation measures have been proposed to urgently enhance rutting resilience in response to climate change.

## 1. Introduction and background

There is a strong consensus that climate change, characterized by global warming, has reached unprecedented scales (Masson-Delmotte et al., 2021). Moreover, global warming is expected to intensify throughout this century (Schuur et al., 2015). Asphalt pavement infrastructure is projected to be significantly affected by climate change, including factors such as temperature, humidity, and sunlight percentage (Swarna et al., 2021; Haslett et al., 2021; Mallick et al., 2018). Previous studies have revealed that temperature is the most significant climate factor leading to pavement deterioration since asphalt mixtures are temperature-sensitive materials (Dawson, 2014; Miao et al., 2022). However, typically, asphalt pavements are designed and maintained based on historical climate data, often overlooking the implications of future climate change, leading to potential issues with accuracy in assessment (Meagher et al., 2012). For example, the current specification for asphalt pavement design in China (JTG D50-2017) requires using at least ten years of historical average temperatures for asphalt pavement design. The default climate inputs for the Mechanistic-Empirical (ME) pavement design model used in the United States rely on average temperatures spanning from 1985 to 2004, a practice that may not fully account for the evolving climate conditions (Chen et al., 2021).

Moreover, given the immense scale of asphalt pavement infrastructure, there is a growing concern regarding the impact of climate change on asphalt pavements. The potential consequences of climate change on asphalt pavements encompass deteriorating pavement performance, shortened lifespan, increased maintenance frequency, higher lifecycle costs, and amplified Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions (Swarna and Hossain, 2022; Piryonesi and El-Diraby, 2021; Qiao et al., 2015). These factors also serve as commonly utilized metrics in literatures for assessing the influence of climate change on asphalt pavements. Ignoring the impact of climate change on pavement would also shorten the lifespan of pavement infrastructure and lead to significant environmental stress accompanied by solid waste (Gu et al., 2023). Underwood et al. (2017) revealed that climate change could lead to a misestimation of asphalt performance grade in 35% of US asphalt pavements. There was also research concluded that climate change would decrease the fatigue life of asphalt pavement (Zhang et al., 2024). Furthermore, Gudipudi et al. (2017) utilized the ME model to estimate the impact of climate change on rutting depth (RD) and fatigue cracking in US asphalt pavements, highlighting a significant adverse effect of climate change on RD. Zhang et al. (2022) established an evaluation index “influence degree of climate change on rutting resilience (d)"

In China, some scholars have investigated the influence of climate change on rutting resistance in several provinces or cities, analyzing variations in impact among different regions (Zhang et al., 2022, 2024).

There is a substantial body of research indicating that designing pavement systems and maintenance strategies based on a static climate assumption may result in more frequent maintenance needs. This, in turn, leads to higher overall lifecycle costs and increased GHG emissions for pavements. For instance, Qiao et al. (2019, 2022) revealed that global warming would shorten the lifespan of asphalt pavements and increase maintenance frequency, and indicated that 1 ◦C temperature increment would result in approximately \$650–700 million per year in additional maintenance costs for 24 research sections in the United States. Furthermore, Underwood et al. (2017) revealed that under the Representative Concentration Pathway (RCP) of 8.5 scenario, which corresponds to a projected increase in radiative forcing of 8.5 W per square meter by 2100, incorrect asphalt grade selection could lead to up to an extra \$35.8 billion in costs for the United States. Scholars have also developed carbon footprint models for the entire lifecycle of asphalt pavements under climate change scenarios, revealing a significant increase in GHG emissions due to climate change (Liu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2020; Ma et al., 2021).

Climate change significantly impacts the performance and overall lifecycle costs of asphalt pavements, highlighting the importance of implementing appropriate adaptation measures. Typical adaptation strategies include reducing pavement temperature, controlling traffic load (N), adjusting asphalt pavement structures, upgrading asphalt performance grade, and optimizing maintenance decision model. For example, Chen et al. (2024) demonstrated that adding thermochromic asphalt could lower pavement temperature, thereby enhancing high-temperature stability. Carroll et al. (2019) revealed that encouraging a shift from private cars to public transport could substantially reduce GHG emissions. Moreover, researchers have proposed requirements for structural parameters like asphalt pavement course thickness and modulus to improve pavement resilience (Liu et al., 2023a; Knott et al., 2019). Studies have also illustrated that using climate data with shorter time spans could more accurately predict asphalt performance grade. Upgrading asphalt performance grade could enhance asphalt pavements’ ability to adapt to climate change (Liu et al., 2023b; Swarna et al., 2022). Additionally, Yao et al. (2024) demonstrated that optimizing maintenance decision models could reduce overall pavement lifecycle costs and alleviate the impacts of climate change.

Overall, previous studies have convincingly demonstrated that future climate change would influence the performance of asphalt pavements, especially RD, as well as their economic lifecycle costs and GHG emissions, while the extent of these impacts varies by region and study. Simultaneously, the critical importance of developing appropriate climate change adaptation measures to enhance the resilience of asphalt pavements and achieve low-carbon objectives was also emphasized. Nonetheless, the present literatures still have certain limitations. Firstly, most existing studies have used the ME pavement design model to investigate the effects of climate change on North American asphalt pavements, with fewer studies focusing on the impact on Chinese asphalt pavements using current Chinese specifications. Secondly, some studies have tended to concentrate on specific regions, whereas China’s expansive territory has exhibited significant climate variations, resulting in notable differences in the sensitivity of pavement performance to climate change across different climatic conditions. Therefore, there is a necessity to categorize climate zones in China and conduct studies to assess the distinct impacts of climate change on pavements in each zone, as well as to develop tailored adaptation strategies accordingly. Lastly, most existing studies have predominantly examined the individual impacts of future climate change on RD, GHG emissions, or adaptation measures, while lacking a holistic and integrated assessment.

Therefore, this study examines the disparities in climate change across different climate zones and delves into how climate change affects RD, d, and maintenance GHG emissions of freeways in each zone. Moreover, it offers targeted measures for adapting freeways to climate change in various climate zones, with the aim of supporting the achievement of sustainable and low-carbon pavements.

## 2. Objective

The main objective of this study is to evaluate the varied impact of future climate change on rutting resilience of freeways in various climatic zones across China, which includes the following specific goals.

● Investigating the future climate change trends.

● Evaluating the differential impact of climate change on pavement RD and d values.

● Assessing the varying impact of climate change on GHG emissions during maintenance.

● Proposing potential measures for pavement adaptation to climate change.

## 3. Methods

The methodological framework of this study, as illustrated in Fig. 1, was divided into six phases. Firstly, future climate data was obtained from the Global Climate Models (GCMs). Secondly, China was segmented into climate zones and future climate trends were forecasted in each zone based on the current specification for asphalt pavement construction in China (JTG F40-2004). Thirdly, changes in RD were predicted based on the JTG D50-2017, and variations among climate zones were analyzed. Fourthly, a resilience assessment model was established to predict changes in the degree of influence of climate change on rutting resilience, and variations among climate zones were compared. Fifthly, maintenance strategy specifically addressing rutting was devised, a GHG emission model was developed, changes in GHG emissions during the maintenance were projected, and differences among climate zones were compared. Finally, potential measures to enhance asphalt pavement resistance to rutting in response to climate change across different climate zones were proposed.

## 3.1. Climate data processing

Climate models are crucial for understanding potential future climate changes, as they intricately simulate interactions among the atmosphere, oceans, and landmasses. The World Climate Research Programme (WCRP) provides numerous GCMs, including five representative concentration pathways (RCPs) at 1.9, 2.6, 4.5, 7.0, and 8.5 scenarios, which represent varying levels of greenhouse gas concentration trajectories, with RCP 1.9 and 2.6 indicating low emissions scenarios, RCP 4.5 a stabilization scenario, RCP 7.0 a high emissions scenario, and RCP 8.5 the most extreme scenario depicting future greenhouse gas emissions. This study exclusively focuses on RCP 8.5, sourced from NASA’s repository of raw climate data, accessible at [https://www.nccs.nasa.gov/](https://www.nccs.nasa.gov/). Programming techniques are employed to meticulously process the raw data, resulting in the development of the asphalt pavement RD estimation model and the identification of key climatic parameters necessary for climate zone classification, including annual average temperature, average temperature of the warmest month, average temperature of the coldest month, and annual lowest temperature.

## 3.2. Climate zones division

Significant disparities exist in climatic conditions across different climate zones in China, which consequently lead to varying sensitivities of rutting performance to climate change. Therefore, it’s imperative to conduct climate zoning in China and analyze the impact of climate change on rutting performance in each zone. According to the JTG F40-2004, China was categorized into 12 climate zones based on the average temperature of the warmest month and annual lowest temperature, as outlined in Table 1.

$$

Where.

$$
\begin{aligned} &RD_{\omega} \; RD_{b} =  RD parameters ,  taken as   10   and   40,  respectively ,   ( mm ); \\&a_{0}, \; a_{I} =  model coefficients ,  taken as   1   and   3,  respectively .\\ \end{aligned}
$$

(7), as shown in Fig. 2(a). ## 3.6. Maintenance strategy and carbon dioxide equivalent emissions calculation model

Pavement rutting significantly impacts the driving comfort and safety of freeways. The JTG D50-2017 stipulates an allowable RD of 15 mm on freeways. Therefore, this study assumes that maintenance is necessary when RD exceeds 15 mm, with the maintenance approach involving milling and resurfacing. The GHG emission model during the maintenance phase includes GHG emissions from the production of raw materials and mixtures, transportation of raw materials and mixtures, and maintenance construction. The calculation of carbon dioxide equivalent $( \mathrm { C O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { e } )$ emissions is illustrated in Eq. (11).

$$
CO_{2}e = 10^{-6} \times (EC_{p} + EC_{t} + EC_{c}) \times CV \times EF \times GWP\tag{11}
$$

Where.

## 4.2. Future equivalent air temperature change

Equivalent air temperature serves as a crucial indicator for predicting the RD of asphalt pavement, closely correlated with the annual average temperature, average temperature of the warmest month and the coldest month, as depicted in Eq. ## 4.4. Rutting resistance change due to climate change

The value of d can be directly reflected by the ability of asphalt pavement to resist rutting under climate change. In Fig. 9, the changes in the value of d in 20 years and 40 years across different climate zones are illustrated. It can be observed from the figure that future climate change will have a significantly adverse impact on rutting resilience, and this adverse effect will be intensified over time. Specifically, in climate zone 3–2, the value of d is as high as 0.592 in 20 years and increases further to 1.378 in 40 years. The climate zones are ranked by their d values as follows: $3 - 2 > 3 - 1 > 3 - 3 > 2 - 3 > 2 - 4 > 2 - 2 > 2 - 1 > 1 - 4 > 3 - 4 ,$ indicating that the greatest impact of climate change on rutting resilience is seen in freeways in climate zone 3–2, while the minimal impact is observed in climate zone 3–4. Additionally, when both the equivalent temperature and d values are considered, it can be seen that zones with higher equivalent temperatures tend to have lower d values, while zones with lower equivalent temperatures have higher d values. This suggests that freeways in lower temperature zones have a weaker ability to resist rutting under climate change, whereas freeways in higher temperature zones have a stronger ability to resist rutting under climate change.
