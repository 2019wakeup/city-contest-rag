## a b s t r a c t

This data article presents information on the measurement of Indirect Tensile Stiffness Modulus of laboratory and field asphalt mixtures. The asphalt mixes are composed of three distinct binders that were categorised by their penetration grade (40/55-TLA, 60/75-TLA, and 60/70-MB) and aggregates (limestone, sharp sand, and filler). The asphalt mixtures are called dense-graded hot mix asphalt (HMA) and gap-graded stone matrix asphalt (SMA). The variables in the dataset were selected in accordance with the specifications of the dynamic modulus models that are currently in use as well as the needs for the quality control and assurance (QC & QA) assessment of asphalt concrete mixes. The data parameters included are temperature, asphalt content, and binder viscosity, air void content, cumulative percent retained on 19, 12.5, and 4.75 mm sieves, maximum theoretical specific gravity, aggregate passing #200 sieve, effective asphalt content, density, flow, marshal stability, coarse-to-fine particle ratio and the Indirect Tensile Stiffness Modulus (ITSM). Utilising soft computing techniques, models were developed utilising the data thus eliminating the requirement for complex and timeconsuming laboratory testing.

## 1. Value of the Data

The dataset utilized for creating soft computing models to forecast the stiffness modulus of bituminous mixes has multiple functions and is significant in diverse domains. Data Scientist in pavement and materials engineering, can use this dataset used to develop soft computing models like artificial neural networks (ANNs), support vector machines (SVMs), fuzzy logic systems, or genetic algorithms, to predict the stiffness modulus of bituminous mixes. The dataset can be utilized by researchers to study and analyse the elements that affect the stiffness modulus of bituminous mixtures, contributing to progress in materials science, civil engineering, and transportation infrastructure. Materials scientists specializing in asphalt and bituminous mixtures can utilize the dataset for research and development to enhance material characteristics and performance.

The data can assist in quality control procedures for bituminous mixture manufacturing by predicting stiffness modulus values to guarantee adherence to standards and requirements. Accurate estimation of stiffness modulus is essential for planning long-lasting and secure transportation infrastructure including roads, highways, and airport runways. Accurate estimation is crucial for selecting the right materials and thicknesses to endure anticipated traffic loads and environmental factors.

Construction businesses can optimize material usage and decrease costs by precisely measuring or estimating stiffness modulus, thus avoiding overdesign or early pavement failures. Safety for motorists, bikers, and pedestrians is enhanced by accurately predicting the stiffness modulus of pavement layers to ensure the structural integrity of roads and highways. Civil engineers specializing in infrastructure design, construction, and maintenance would use the dataset and models to guarantee the structural integrity and durability of transportation networks. Construction managers can utilize the dataset to enhance resource allocation, scheduling, and quality control for road construction projects, leading to project success and durability of the infrastructure.

## 2. Background

The elastic stiffness modulus is a crucial determinant of the performance of asphalt concrete (AC) pavements [1]. Pavement design approaches necessitate the inclusion of the elastic stiffness strength of materials. Hence, by characterising the qualities of asphalt concrete materials in terms of elastic stiffness, it is possible to enhance the design and quality capabilities of the pavement structure [2]. The elasticity strength characteristic of asphalt concrete may vary depending on the region and may be described as either elastic stiffness or dynamic modulus. The methods for measuring this strength are conducted according to the commonly utilised dynamic modulus approach in the United States. The BS EN 12,697-26 test, known as Indirect Tensile Stiffness Modulus (ITSM), is utilised by the UK and several Caribbean countries. Several studies have been conducted on prediction models for dynamic modulus testing. However, there is a lack of research on predicting ITSM, with only very limited number of papers available. Hence, the purpose of this work is to provide a dataset that can be utilised to construct a prediction model for the Indirect Tensile Stiffness Modulus of both Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) and Stone Matrix Asphalt (SMA) mixes. The advancement of such models obviates the need for intricate, costly, and time-consuming laboratory experimentation.

## 4. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Trinidad Lake Asphalt and the municipal road agency in Trinidad were the sources from which the bitumen components were obtained. The National Quarries Company Ltd., which is a public government quarry in Trinidad, was the source of the mineral aggregates that were utilized in the production of asphalt concrete mixes.

The asphalt concrete compositions were obtained from a plant that produces asphalt in Trinidad, as well as from the highway and transportation laboratory that is located under the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of the West Indies in Trinidad and Tobago. The Marshall Mix Design approach was utilized in the process of formulating the combination. The samples were made up of mixes that were derived from twelve distinct sorts of mixtures. The mixes consisted of two HMA and SMA mixtures that were constructed with three different types of binder: 40/55 TLA, 60/75/ TLA, and 60/70 modified polymer.
